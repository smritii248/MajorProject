# Bagmati River Textile Waste — Extra Contribution Analysis

## Purpose
Section 1.1 of the EcoThread proposal states that the Bagmati River system
"has been repeatedly documented as significantly polluted by discarded
textiles" (citing the Department of Environment's Bagmati River Pollution
Status Report, 2023), but does not quantify this claim. This analysis
develops that single sentence into an estimated figure, and connects it to
EcoThread's potential real-world impact.

## Method
No Nepal-specific study quantifies the textile-specific share of Kathmandu
Valley's municipal solid waste, or how much of that waste specifically
reaches the Bagmati River. In the absence of local data, this analysis
combines three publicly available figures with one explicitly stated
assumption:

1. **City-wide waste volume**: Kathmandu Valley generates approximately
   1,200 tonnes of solid waste per day.
2. **River leakage rate**: approximately 25% of solid waste in the valley
   is dumped on roads or riverbanks and ultimately reaches open waterways,
   including the Bagmati.
3. **Textile fraction (assumed)**: no Nepal-specific figure exists for what
   share of municipal solid waste is textile material. International waste
   characterization studies commonly estimate this at 2–5%. This analysis
   presents a low (2%), mid (3.5%), and high (5%) estimate rather than a
   single number, to make the uncertainty explicit.

## Results

| Estimate | Daily textile waste (city-wide) | Daily reaching Bagmati | Annual reaching Bagmati |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low (2%) | 24.0 tonnes | 6.0 tonnes | 2,190 tonnes/year |
| Mid (3.5%) | 42.0 tonnes | 10.5 tonnes | 3,832.5 tonnes/year |
| High (5%) | 60.0 tonnes | 15.0 tonnes | 5,475 tonnes/year |

## Connecting to EcoThread

Using the mid estimate (3,832.5 tonnes/year of textile waste plausibly
reaching the Bagmati), we modeled two adoption scenarios for EcoThread's
potential diversion impact:

| Adoption scenario | Estimated diversion |
|---|---|
| Conservative (1% of waste stream diverted) | ~38.3 tonnes/year |
| Moderate (5% of waste stream diverted) | ~191.6 tonnes/year |

These figures represent the estimated volume of textile waste that could be
kept out of the Bagmati River system if EcoThread achieved the modeled
adoption rate among Kathmandu Valley residents, by redirecting garments that
would otherwise be discarded toward resale, donation, or recycling instead.

## Limitations

- The 2–5% textile-fraction assumption is drawn from international waste
  studies, not Nepal-specific data. This is consistent with the proposal's
  own stated gap: "No Nepal specific textile waste lifecycle dataset exists
  at the household or garment level" (Section 1.1).
- The 25% river-leakage rate is a valley-wide average across all waste
  types; it may not hold uniformly for textiles specifically, which behave
  differently in dumping/burning practices than organic or plastic waste.
- Adoption rate estimates (1%, 5%) are illustrative scenarios, not
  predictions based on user research; an actual adoption rate would depend
  on marketing reach, trust in AI grading, and infrastructure availability,
  as explored in the proposed 30–50 participant behavioural study
  (Chapter 4).

## Conclusion

While no precise figure exists for textile-specific pollution in the
Bagmati River, combining available city-wide waste data with explicitly
stated assumptions produces a defensible estimated range of 2,190–5,475
tonnes of textile waste reaching the river annually. Even under a
conservative adoption scenario, EcoThread could plausibly divert tens of
tonnes of textile waste per year from this pathway — providing a concrete,
locally-grounded rationale for the system's environmental relevance beyond
the single supporting sentence originally included in the proposal.
